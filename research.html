  3. Peek-a-Book: Who Hides in the Limit Order Book, and When?" (FMA 2026 semifinalist in \textit{Market Microstructure})
    4. View Abstract
    This paper examines how uncertainty affects liquidity provision by incorporating macro, valuation, and execution uncertainties in a limit-order-book model. The model yields a spread decomposition and a hidden-share condition: hidden liquidity increases with macro uncertainty but decreases with valuation uncertainty because reduced time priority of hidden orders raises non-execution and selection costs. The macro effect is heterogeneous in equilibrium: when macro uncertainty is high, liquidity providers provide hidden liquidity to easier-to-value stocks and remove from harder-to-value stocks to minimize overall portfolio exposure. Consistent with these predictions, I show that increases in macro uncertainty raise hidden liquidity for easier-to-value firms and reduce it for harder-to-value firms. Robustness tests with alternative proxies and exogenous shocks from the COVID-19 pandemic and the temporary suspension of NYSE floor trading support a causal interpretation.

  8. Inside a Structural Disagreement Measure: Composition, Validation, and Decay
    9. View Abstract
    Investor disagreement is unobservable and its proxies correlate weakly, so a recent literature builds model-implied composites whose internal composition is set by theory rather than by fit. That composition has never been examined, because the latent variable such a measure estimates was itself unobservable. This paper shows that the DIS measure of Goulding et al. (2026, JFE) is algebraically invertible: released under five risk-aversion settings, its two structural blocks and the latent belief-precision term solve exactly, and three restrictions the mapping imposes hold against I/B/E/S, Compustat and CRSP data the recovery never uses, at correlations above 0.91. The composition is lopsided. One block carries 98.6% of the variance, and the analyst inputs---the only ones derived from what an identifiable population says about a specific quantity---supply 41% of belief precision in the lowest-disagreement decile but 13% in the highest, leaving the measure nearly inactive as a measure of beliefs precisely where it earns its return predictability. Those returns are themselves fragile. The 21.5% annualized alpha falls to 4 to $7% against realized volatility, and that residual is confined to 1994--2008: over 2009--2022 idiosyncratic volatility spans the composite rather than the reverse, while every compositional finding replicates in both halves.
